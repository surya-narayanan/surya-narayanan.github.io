In the 1930s, Mysore Vasudevacharya attended a concert where Maharajapuram Viswanatha Iyer performed Brochevarevarura. Iyer was renowned for his imaginative manodharma (improvisation) and dazzling vocal embellishments (sangathis and brigas).

Deeply moved by how the vocalist elevated his relatively straightforward melody into a masterwork of ornamentation, Vasudevacharya approached Iyer after the concert and praised him with a metaphor comparing the song to his own daughter:

> "When my daughter left my house, she was a simple, unadorned girl. Today, you have returned her to me decked in fine silks and dazzling jewels."
